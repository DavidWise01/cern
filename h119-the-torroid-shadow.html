H119 — The Torroid Shadow

WHY is only the z=0 plane indefinite? The torroid body (408 digits) is ~10^80 larger than brace (328d) or box (329d). Its shadow falls on exactly one plane.

Panel 1 — The Question

H118 showed that of the three coordinate-plane restrictions of Q3p, only the z=0 plane is indefinite (adj[2][2] < 0, while adj[0][0] and adj[1][1] are positive). But WHY?

THE DECOMPOSITION:
  adj[i][i] = Q[j][j] · Q[k][k]  −  Q[j][k]²
              ═══════════════════     ════════
                  PRODUCT              SQUARE

  DEFINITE when PRODUCT > SQUARE (product wins)
  INDEFINITE when SQUARE > PRODUCT (square wins)

THE THREE RACES:
  adj[0][0]: (box·torroid)   vs (box⊗torroid)²    — kills brace
  adj[1][1]: (brace·torroid) vs (brace⊗torroid)²  — kills box
  adj[2][2]: (brace·box)     vs (brace⊗box)²      — kills torroid

When the torroid (408d) participates in the PRODUCT,
it adds ~80 digits of magnitude → product wins easily.
When it's ABSENT (adj[2][2]), the race is essentially a tie
decided by leading digits — and the SQUARE wins by 0.0016%.

Toddler Version — The Biggest Kid on the Team

Imagine three kids on a see-saw team. Two of them (Brace and Box) are about the same size. The third one (Torroid) is a GIANT — like, a hundred-billion-billion-billion-billion-billion-billion-billion-billion times bigger.

Now we play a game: take one kid away and see if the see-saw stays balanced.

Take away Brace (the smallest)? Torroid is so heavy it holds everything steady. Balanced!

Take away Box (medium)? Torroid still holds it. Balanced!

Take away Torroid (the giant)? Now it's just Brace and Box, who are almost the SAME size. The see-saw wobbles back and forth because neither one is heavy enough to hold it steady. Wobbly!

That wobbly see-saw IS the z=0 plane being indefinite. The giant's shadow — the place where things go wobbly when the giant steps away — that's what we measured. It's exactly 85 digits wide.

And the wobble is SO close to balanced: the difference between balanced and wobbly is just 0.0016%. Like, if the see-saw was a mile long, the difference would be less than an inch.
