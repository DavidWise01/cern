Panel 1 — What We Asked

H153 proved c = 0 is FORBIDDEN on the null cone (R01 not a perfect square). All roots must have c ≥ 1. This instrument computes Q3p(a, b, 1) — the c = 1 slice — to find the smallest lattice point that can reach Q3p = 0 through the ternary form's extra torroid coupling terms.

Q3p(a, b, 1) = Q00·a² + 2Q01·ab + Q11·b² + 2Q02·a + 2Q12·b + Q22

Treat Q3p(a, b, 1) = 0 as a quadratic in a at fixed b. The discriminant Δ(b) = 4(Ab² + 2Bb + C) where:

Since A > 0, D_min < 0 → the discriminant parabola crosses zero at two points, creating a FORBIDDEN ZONE between them where Q3p(a, b, 1) has no real roots in a.

Panel 6 — What the 21 Roots Are (Reinterpretation)

The 21-Root Theorem states: the minimum word length in the frozen lattice F is exactly 21. This means F contains no vectors shorter than 21 steps.

These shortest vectors are NOT on the null cone Q3p = 0 (which needs ~10⁴² steps). They are the shortest vectors in F in the ℓ¹ norm |a| + |b| + |c|, regardless of their Q3p value.

Geometrically: F is a sublattice of Z³, and the 21 roots are its first nonzero representatives. They sit DEEP in the negative zone of Q3p — all 21 have Q3p < 0 (since the form is negative-definite for small vectors at any c). The null cone is astronomically far away.

The connection between Q3p and the 21 roots is not through the null cone. It is through the LATTICE STRUCTURE: Q3p defines which sublattice vectors belong to F, and the minimum word length 21 is a measure of how "sparse" F is in the full lattice Z³.

VERDICT: THE 21 ROOTS ARE NOT NULL-CONE VECTORS

The c=1 saddle point (a~10⁴¹, b~10⁴¹) has Q3p = −408d. The discriminant is negative in a 42-digit window, and the null cone is reachable only at word length ~10⁴². The 21-Root Theorem's minimum word length 21 is 10⁴⁰ orders shorter than the nearest null-cone point. The 21 roots are shortest vectors in the frozen lattice F, NOT null-cone vectors. Q3p connects to F through lattice structure, not through vanishing.
