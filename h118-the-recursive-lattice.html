Toddler Version — Boxes Inside Boxes

Imagine a tic-tac-toe board — 9 squares in a 3×3 grid. The middle square is special: it's the boss (we call it the "constant" or the "focus"). The 8 squares around it are workers (we call them "variables").

Now here's the magic part: if you zoom into any square, you find ANOTHER tic-tac-toe board inside it! Each small square has its own boss and 8 workers. And if you zoom into THOSE squares, you find MORE boards inside. Boxes inside boxes inside boxes — that's recursion!

The really cool thing: when you zoom in TWO levels, the pattern looks exactly like the original, just with bigger numbers. It's like looking at a reflection of a reflection — you see yourself again!

The signs on the squares make a checkerboard pattern: minus, plus, minus / plus, minus, plus / minus, plus, minus. Just like a chess board! And after zooming in through recursion, the adjugate (first zoom) is almost all plus signs, with only ONE minus — in the bottom-right corner (that's the z=0 plane that H117 found was "indefinite").

The bottom line: the magic lines where the form equals zero (from H117) only exist in ONE of the three flat planes (the z=0 plane). The other two planes? No magic lines at all — they're locked tight.

IMPROVEMENT RECOMMENDATION → H119: The Torroid Shadow

H118 proves zeros exist in ONLY the z=0 plane — the torroid's shadow. H119 would investigate WHY: what property of the torroid body (body 2, 408 digits, the largest) causes the z=0 restriction to be indefinite while the other two are definite? The adjugate shows adj[0][0] and adj[1][1] are positive (736d, 735d) while adj[2][2] is negative (651d) — the gap is 85 digits. The torroid's excess magnitude (408 vs 328-329) creates an asymmetry that makes exactly one plane soft.

Growth: ~ 4^n / √(πn) — the number of ways to walk n steps
right and n steps left on a line. The irrational barrier says no such
walk reaches the zero line in Q3p's xy-plane.
