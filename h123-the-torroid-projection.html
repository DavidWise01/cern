Panel 1 — The Question

H122 found the eigenvector DIRECTIONS — the saddle is tilted 29.35 degrees, with brace dominating the thin (collapse) direction at 76 percent. But H122 also found that the torroid coupling projections (693d, 696d) dwarf both eigenvalues (324d, 329d) by hundreds of orders of magnitude.

The question: does the torroid PREFER one eigenvector over the other? Project Q3p into the rotated basis {v₁ thin, v₂ wide, e₃ torroid}. The off-diagonal entries Q'[0][2] and Q'[1][2] are the BRIDGES between the torroid and the two eigenvectors. Their ratio reveals which direction the torroid pushes hardest.

Panel 9 — Toddler Version

Remember the Pringle chip from last time? It has a thin side (could break) and a wide side (strong). And the enormous TORROID elephant sitting next to it?

Now we asked: when the elephant leans on the Pringle, which side does it lean on?

Answer: the elephant leans on the STRONG side. Three hundred and seventy-eight times harder than it pushes on the weak side.

This is good news for the Pringle! The thin weak side — the part that could break — the elephant barely touches it. The elephant is basically a bodyguard protecting the strong side, making it even stronger.

The weak side is on its own. The torroid does not help it, but it does not hurt it either.

IMPROVEMENT RECOMMENDATION → H124: The Effective Mass


H123 shows the torroid couples 378 times harder to the wide (stable) axis than to the thin (collapse) axis. But coupling strength alone does not tell you whether the torroid makes the near-degeneracy quantitatively better or worse.


H124 would compute the SCHUR COMPLEMENT of the rotated Q' with respect to the torroid block. The Schur complement "integrates out" the torroid — it gives you a 2 by 2 EFFECTIVE matrix for the brace-box subspace that includes the torroid's indirect influence. The effective thin eigenvalue would reveal whether the 378-to-1 asymmetry actually shifts the near-collapse threshold, and by how many digits.
