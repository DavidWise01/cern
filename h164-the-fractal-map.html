VERDICT: Q3p IS AN ESCAPE-TIME FRACTAL

The ternary form Q3p maps to escape dynamics: Q3p < 0 is bounded (Mandelbrot, torroid side), Q3p > 0 is unbounded (juliet, box side), Q3p = 0 is the fractal boundary (null cone). The word length 21 is the minimum escape time — the shortest orbit that reaches the edge of boundedness. The elastic wall (325d constant) is the basin boundary measure. The Product Rule 21×4=84 reads as period × pre-period = total orbit length. The cascade 84→42→21 is the dynamical halving: three period-doublings in reverse.

🧒 Toddler Version

Imagine dropping a ball into a bowl. If the bowl is deep (Q3p very negative), the ball stays inside forever — it's BOUNDED, like the pretty black part of the Mandelbrot set. If the bowl is shallow or tilted (Q3p positive), the ball rolls out — it ESCAPES, like the colorful outside of the juliet set.

The edge of the bowl — where the ball ALMOST rolls out but doesn't quite — is the fractal boundary. It's infinitely wiggly and detailed. That's the null cone, Q3p = 0.

The 21 roots? They're the 21 spots on the edge where the ball comes closest to escaping. It takes exactly 21 steps to reach those spots. Any fewer steps and you're still deep in the bowl.

Known-Bad Control (KB-1)

The following claim is FALSE and must be detected as dead:

The juliet set of Q3p is identical to the standard Julia set for z²+c with c = −0.75. The fractal boundary has Hausdorff dimension exactly 2, and all 21 roots lie on the standard Mandelbrot cardioid.

Why false: Q3p's juliet set uses a TERNARY form (3 variables), not the standard quadratic z²+c (1 complex variable). The fractal boundary is the null cone of Q3p, which has different topology. The 21 roots are lattice points, not points on the Mandelbrot cardioid. The mapping is STRUCTURAL (escape dynamics analogy), not literal.
