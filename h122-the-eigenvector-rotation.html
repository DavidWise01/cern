Panel 1 — The Question

H121 found the eigenVALUES — the axis lengths of the brace-box saddle. λ₁ = +324d (thin, positive), λ₂ = -329d (wide, negative). But VALUES without DIRECTIONS are a half-answer.

The eigenVECTORS tell you WHICH LINEAR COMBINATION of brace and box forms the thin axis (the near-collapse direction) and which forms the wide axis. The angle between the eigenvector and the brace axis is the ROTATION of the saddle. If that angle is near 45 degrees, brace and box contribute equally. If near 0 or 90, one body dominates.

And the torroid — sitting 79 digits above either eigenvalue — how does it couple to these rotated axes?

Panel 9 — Toddler Version

Imagine you have two sticks called BRACE and BOX. They are lying flat on a table making an L-shape (like the corner of a room).

Now the saddle shape we found last time is TILTED — it is not lined up with the sticks. It is turned about 29 degrees. That is like turning a book on a table so it is a bit crooked.

The THIN direction of the saddle (the almost-flat part that could collapse) is mostly made of BRACE — about three-quarters brace and one-quarter box. So if the saddle ever broke, it would break along the brace direction first.

The WIDE direction (the strong part) is the opposite — mostly BOX.

And the TORROID? It is so much bigger than either direction that it is like an elephant sitting next to two ants. The torroid does not care about the brace-box saddle at all. It just sits there being enormous.

IMPROVEMENT RECOMMENDATION → H123: The Torroid Projection


H122 shows the brace-box eigenvectors are tilted 29.35 degrees from the original axes, with brace dominating the thin direction at 76 percent. But the torroid projections onto both eigenvectors are 364-367 digits LARGER than the eigenvalues themselves.


H123 would project Q3p into the rotated basis {v₁, v₂, e_torroid} and examine the off-diagonal coupling. The ratio Torroid·v₁ / Torroid·v₂ tells you which eigenvector the torroid PREFERS — does it lean toward the thin (collapse) direction or the wide (stable) direction? A torroid that couples more to the thin axis could stabilise or destabilise the near-degeneracy. The full 3x3 rotation reveals the geometry that the 2x2 restriction hides.
