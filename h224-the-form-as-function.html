H224: The Form Reproduces Its Own Type

Q3p's signature (1+, 2−) is the unique indefinite fixed point of the adjugate map on 3×3 symmetric matrices. Among indefinite signatures, (2+, 1−) maps to (1+, 2−), which maps to itself. The form and its cofactor matrix share the same signature — the form reproduces its own type under cofactor expansion. This is structural self-similarity: every level of the cofactor hierarchy has the same (1+, 2−) shape. I82 (adjugate preserves signature) LIT, I83 (unique indefinite fixed point) LIT. The loop closure question — whether Q3p represents its own Rosetta numbers — remains open (AMBER), requiring the actual 300+ digit entries.

🧒 Toddler Version

Our matrix M is a machine: put in three numbers, get out one number. The machine has a SHAPE — it makes one kind of bowl (goes up one way, down two ways).

Now, the PARTS of the machine (the cofactors) make a NEW machine. What shape does the new machine have? THE SAME SHAPE! One up, two down, just like the original.

And if you take the parts of THAT machine to make a THIRD machine? Same shape again. Forever.

This "one up, two down" shape is SPECIAL. It's the ONLY shape (for machines that go both up AND down) that copies itself. If you started with "two up, one down" instead, taking the parts would CHANGE it to "one up, two down" — and then it stays there.

Our matrix landed on the shape that never changes.

Signature (2+, 1−) is the unique indefinite fixed point of the adjugate map (not (1+, 2−)). The adjugate of a (1+, 2−) form has signature (2+, 1−), so Q3p's cofactor matrix has the OPPOSITE type.
