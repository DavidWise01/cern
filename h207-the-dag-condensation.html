Panel 4 — The Roster Is a Selection, Not a Closure

  IS THE ROSETTA ROSTER CLOSED UNDER ANY OPERATION?

  Under +:   17 of 120 pairs land in roster, 103 don't. NO.
  Under ×:   12 of 120 pairs land in roster, 108 don't. NO.

  IS IT A CLOSURE OF {2,3}?

  {2,3} under + generates ALL integers ≥ 2.
  {2,3} under +,× generates ALL positive integers.
  The closure would be INFINITE, not 15 numbers.

  THE ROSTER IS NOT A CLOSURE. IT IS A SELECTION.

  The 15 numbers are those combinations of {2,3} that have
  MEANING in Q3p's structure: rank, bodies, entries, gap, lock,
  escape, Smith invariant, word count, master product, etc.

  × and ² ALONE from {2,3} reach: {2,3,4,6,8,18,36} = 7 of 15.
  ADDITION is essential for: {10,17,21,25,42,84,289,336} = 8 of 15.
  (17 needs subtraction: 21−4=17; 289 needs 17 first.)

  THE SPLIT:
  MULTIPLICATIVE CORE (×,² from seeds): 7 numbers
  ADDITIVE EXTENSIONS (+,− from core):   8 numbers
  7 + 8 = 15.
  The core is built by multiplication. The extensions by addition.

    Most essential children in the dependency DAG.
    4 numbers MUST be built from 6: {10, 18, 36, 42}.

  RELATIONSHIP:
    6 feeds into 21 (through 18: 3×6=18, 3+18=21).
    21 feeds into 42 (through the gap: but actually
    42 = 6+36 = 6+6², bypassing 21 entirely!).

    42 = 6+36 = 6+6². Its essential parents are {6, 36}.
    21 is NOT an essential parent of 42.
    21 feeds 17 and 25, not 42.
    The gap doesn't depend on the hub in the generation order.

  CAUTION: 42 = 2×21 is an ALTERNATIVE path at layer 4.
  But 42 is first reachable at layer 3 via 6+36.
  The 2×21 path appears a layer later.
  So 21 is an accidental parent of 42, not essential.

H207: 20 Essential Edges, 1 Orphan, 2 Hubs

The condensed DAG has 20 essential edges. 6 numbers have unique paths (no alternative): 6, 18, 36, 17, 336, 289. The only orphan is 84 (zero essential parents — two fully independent paths: 2×42 or 4×21). The DAG hub is 6 (entries), with 4 essential children, different from the Rosetta hub 21. Surprise: 42 doesn't need 21 in the DAG (42=6+36 at layer 3; 2×21 is a later alternative). The roster is NOT a closure of {2,3} — it's a SELECTION of the 15 combinations that have meaning in Q3p. The multiplicative core (×,² from seeds) produces 7 numbers; addition extends to all 15. I48 LIT.

🧒 Toddler Version

Remember the recipe for building all 15 numbers from 2 and 3? Today we asked: for each number, is there ONLY ONE way to build it, or can you get there by different routes?

Some numbers have only ONE road — if you block it, you can't get there at all. Like the number 6: the ONLY way is 2×3. Block that and 6 is gone forever.

One number has NO required road — the number 84 can be built TWO completely different ways (2×42 or 4×21), and neither way is required. It's like a town with two bridges — knock down either one and you can still get there.

The number 6 is the busiest intersection — four other numbers MUST be built from 6. Take away 6 and you lose almost everything.

And here's the surprise: the number 42 (the big star!) doesn't actually NEED 21 (the other star!) to be built. 42 = 6 + 36, and both of those come from 6 alone. The two stars are independent!

Known-Bad Control (KB-1)

The following claim is FALSE and must be detected as dead:

All 15 Rosetta numbers have unique shortest paths with no alternatives, making the DAG fully deterministic. The number 84 has three essential parents (2, 4, and 42), and the number 42 essentially depends on 21 as its sole path is 2 times 21 at layer 2. The Rosetta roster is algebraically closed under addition, with every sum of two roster members also being a roster member.

Only 6 have unique paths; 84 has ZERO essential parents (not three); 42's first path is 6+36 at layer 3, not 2×21; roster is NOT closed under addition (103 of 120 sums miss).

The two-hundred-and-seventh instrument pared the recipe to its bones. Twenty edges that cannot be removed. Six paths that have no detour. One orphan that sits at the junction of two independent roads, needing neither. The entries — six, the third triangular number, the bridge between families — turn out to carry the most weight in the dependency graph, feeding four numbers that nothing else can produce. Meanwhile the hub that the Rosetta Stone put at the center, twenty-one, turns out to be unneeded by the very number it was supposed to explain. Forty-two does not depend on twenty-one in the generation order. It depends on six and thirty-six, both born from six. The stone's hub and the DAG's hub are different numbers, doing different work, in the same fifteen-number house. And the roster itself is not a mathematical closure but a selection: the fifteen points where seed arithmetic and Q3p structure happen to agree. Not inevitable. Not arbitrary. Chosen by the form.
