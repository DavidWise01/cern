The desert exists because w is an INTEGER. There is no w = 0.5. Either a root has w = 0 (family 1, cheap) or w ≥ 1 (family 2, expensive). The jump from w=0 to w=1 costs 10⁴² in the u-coordinate because |λ_torroid|/λ_crack ≈ 10⁸⁵, and u_min = √(10⁸⁵) = 10⁴².

85/2 = 42: The 85-digit gap from H138 divides by 2 (square root) to give the 42-digit threshold. The 42 minus 3 (family 1 scale) = 39-digit desert. The gap cascade continues: 85 → 42 → 39.

Panel 6 — The Binary Subform

In the w=0 plane, Q3p reduces to a binary quadratic form:

Q_2D(a,b) = Q00·a² + 2Q01·ab + Q11·b²
det(Q_2D) = M01 = −651d (INDEFINITE)

An indefinite binary form has INFINITELY many roots.
The 18 constructive roots are the 18 SHORTEST primitive roots.
The binary form alone gives unlimited Family 1 roots.

But the TERNARY form needs ALL root classes.
18 come from w=0 (the binary subform).
3 more come from w>0 (the full ternary structure).
21 total — the minimum word length of F.

Panel 9 — Toddler Version: Two Neighborhoods

🧒 Imagine a narrow tunnel (the null cone). Inside the tunnel, there are houses (lattice points = roots). The houses come in two neighborhoods.

Neighborhood 1 is right at the entrance, very close. 18 houses, easy to find, easy to visit. These are the constructive roots — we know exactly where each one is.

Then there's a HUGE EMPTY STRETCH — 39 orders of magnitude of nothing. No houses at all. Just desert.

Neighborhood 2 is waaaay at the other end. 3 houses. We know they're there (a mathematician named Eichler proved it), but the desert is so long we can't walk there to check. The road is blocked by a 1056-digit number we can't factor.

Why the desert? Because the tunnel gets NARROWER in one direction (the torroid direction — 10⁻⁴² radians). To reach Neighborhood 2, you have to go through that narrow part, and the first house there is 10⁴² steps away. Nothing can live in between — the lattice dots simply don't reach the tunnel walls.
