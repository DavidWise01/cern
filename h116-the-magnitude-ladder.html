H116 — The Magnitude Ladder

Where is Q3p stiff and where is it soft? Decompose the quadratic form into its 6 terms and map the cancellation hierarchy.

Panel 1 — The Question

H115 found ZERO non-trivial lattice points but revealed structure: the closest approaches to zero (327 digits) all lie in the z=0 plane. Why?

The quadratic form Q(v) = Σ Q[i][j]·v[i]·v[j] has 6 terms at any vector v = (x, y, z):

T1 = Q[0][0]·x²       self-coupling, body X (brace)     328 digits base
T2 = Q[1][1]·y²       self-coupling, body Y (box)       328 digits base
T3 = Q[2][2]·z²       self-coupling, body Z (torroid)   408 digits base
T4 = 2·Q[0][1]·x·y   cross-coupling, X↔Y               328 digits base
T5 = 2·Q[0][2]·x·z   cross-coupling, X↔Z               367 digits base
T6 = 2·Q[1][2]·y·z   cross-coupling, Y↔Z               367 digits base

The magnitude ladder sorts these by |size| at each vector and shows how much cancellation occurs. A "soft direction" is one where large terms nearly cancel. A "stiff direction" is one where a dominant term has nothing to cancel against.

Toddler Version — The See-Saw Game

Imagine you have 6 people trying to balance a giant see-saw. Three sit on one side (the self-couplings — how heavy each body is by itself). Three sit on the other (the cross-couplings — how the bodies pull on each other).

When z=0 (body Z is turned off), only 3 people are on the see-saw. They're all about the same weight (~328 digits heavy). They ALMOST balance — the see-saw tips just a tiny bit (327 digits of tilt). That's the soft direction — the see-saw nearly balances.

When z≠0 (body Z is turned on), person #3 (the Z self-coupling) is a GIANT — 408 digits heavy. Nobody else is big enough to balance them. The see-saw SLAMS down on their side. That's the stiff direction — the see-saw can't balance no matter what.

The magnitude ladder shows exactly who weighs what at every angle of the see-saw.

IMPROVEMENT RECOMMENDATION → H117: The Soft Subspace

H116 maps stiff vs. soft directions. H117 would characterize the SOFT SUBSPACE completely: the z=0 plane where Q3p reduces to a 2×2 form in (x,y). Compute eigenvalues of that 2×2 restriction, find its minimum directions, and determine if any rational direction in that plane gives anomalously low |Q(v)|. This is the natural follow-up — if lattice points exist anywhere accessible, they're in the soft subspace.
