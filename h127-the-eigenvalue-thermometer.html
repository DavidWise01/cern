Panel 9 — Toddler Version

Imagine two strings on a guitar. One string is HUGE — as thick as your arm. The other string is TINY — thinner than a hair.

When you pluck the guitar, the big string makes most of the sound. The little string adds just a tiny hum — 3 parts per million of the big string's sound.

We wanted to know: how tiny IS the tiny string compared to the big one? So we built a THERMOMETER — a ruler that shows how tiny things are in nature.

On this ruler, the atom's color (fine structure) is at 10⁻⁴. The atom's wobble (Lamb shift) is at 10⁻⁶. Our tiny string sits RIGHT BETWEEN THEM — at about 10⁻⁵·⁵.

That means our Pringle chip has one strong direction and one direction that is 3 millionths as strong. It is NOT that the two directions are almost the same — one is a GIANT and one is a MOUSE.

The elephant (torroid) makes the giant even MORE giant compared to the mouse. But it also makes the whole thing harder to break (that was H126's finding).
