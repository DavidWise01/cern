Remember the 8 light-switch combinations? Addresses 0 through 7.

I wanted to know: does the "skip the first 2" rule work for ANY number of switches?

NO! It ONLY works for 3 switches. With 4 switches you get 16 combinations, skip 2 gives 14, but entries would be 10. 14 ≠ 10.

This is the THIRD time we found something that only works for 3. Three is special:

→ A cube with 3 dimensions has 6 faces = 6 entries. Only for 3.

→ 3 × 2 = 6 entries. Only for 3.

→ 2³ − 2 = 6 entries. Only for 3.

Three different reasons all give the SAME answer, and they only agree when the number of switches is 3. Three is the magic number.

But here's the cool part: when you DO have 3 switches, every special number (2,3,4,5,6,7) IS its own address. The number 5 lives at address 5. The number 7 lives at address 7. The name IS the address.
