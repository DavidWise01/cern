TODDLER CORNER

When two protons smash together very fast, sometimes two heavy particles called muons fly out. We weigh them together (that's the "invariant mass"). When we see the same weight over and over, it means a heavier particle was hiding inside for just a moment before it popped into the two muons.

The bumps on the chart are those hiding particles: J/ψ (made of charm quarks, ~3.1 GeV), Υ (bottom quarks, ~9.5 GeV), and Z (carries the weak force, ~91 GeV).

Each one lives at a different size on the "scale ladder" — the Z is the smallest (10-18 m), the J/ψ and Υ are a bit bigger (10-17 m). All are WAY smaller than an atom (10-10 m).

The three-bracket address stamps each collision with WHEN it happened (calendar + clock) and WHERE the detector was pointing (angle). Earth spins 15 arcseconds every clock second.
