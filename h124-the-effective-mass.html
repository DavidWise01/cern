Panel 1 — The Question

H123 showed the torroid couples 378 times harder to the wide (stable) axis than to the thin (collapse) axis. But coupling strength is not the same as effect. Does the torroid actually shift the near-degeneracy? By how many digits?

The SCHUR COMPLEMENT integrates out the torroid. It gives a 2 by 2 EFFECTIVE matrix for the brace-box subspace that includes the torroid's indirect influence. The formula: S = A - B·D⁻¹·Bᵀ, where A is the 2 by 2 brace-box block, D is the torroid self-coupling, and B is the bridge column.

Panel 9 — Toddler Version

Remember the bodyguard elephant from H123? It leans 378 times harder on the strong side of the Pringle.

Now we asked: does the elephant's weight actually CHANGE the shape of the Pringle?

Answer: barely. The elephant pushes both sides a tiny bit — the strong side gets pushed a tiny bit weaker, and the thin side gets pushed a tiny bit stronger. But the pushes are so small compared to the Pringle itself that the Pringle keeps its exact same shape.

The interesting part: the elephant's weight creates a NEW CONNECTION between the two sides of the Pringle that was not there before. Like the elephant sitting in the middle connects the left side to the right side through its body. But even this new connection does not change the Pringle's shape.

The Pringle is TOUGH. The elephant is big but gentle.

IMPROVEMENT RECOMMENDATION → H125: The Cross-Coupling Engine


H124 found that the torroid mediates a 981-digit cross-coupling between the thin and wide eigenvectors — 325 digits larger than the original residual (656d). This cross-coupling is comparable to the diagonal entries themselves (980d and 986d).


H125 would diagonalise the 2 by 2 effective Schur complement matrix to find the EFFECTIVE eigenvectors. How much does the torroid-mediated cross-coupling rotate the eigenvectors compared to the original H122 directions? If the rotation is small (less than a degree), the torroid is truly invisible. If it is large, the torroid reshapes the collapse direction even though it preserves the eigenvalue magnitudes.
