Panel 1 — Question

Can we prove no lattice points exist on the null cone between u ≈ 10³ and u ≈ 10⁴²?

Answer: NO — but we can prove something better and correct H150 in the process.

The desert in H150 was overstated. w=0 roots (Family 1) can have arbitrarily large u — there is no upper bound. The provable statement is a one-sided FLOOR: w≥1 roots cannot have |u| below 10⁴². The correction is itself a finding: the desert is about which FAMILY a root belongs to, not about which u-values are populated.

Panel 2 — The Desert Lower Bound (PROVED)

THEOREM. For any v ∈ Z³ with Q3p(v) = 0 and w-component ≥ 1
(in the eigenvector basis):

    |u| ≥ √(|λ_torroid| / λ_crack) ≈ 10⁴²

PROOF.
  From the null cone equation:
    λ_crack · u² = |λ_bond| · v² + |λ_torroid| · w²
                 ≥ |λ_torroid| · w²     (since |λ_bond|·v² ≥ 0)
                 ≥ |λ_torroid|           (since w ≥ 1)
  Therefore:
    u² ≥ |λ_torroid| / λ_crack ≈ 10⁸⁵
    |u| ≥ 10⁴²                          ∎

The bound is TIGHT: achieved when v = 0, w = 1.

Panel 5 — Root Classes vs Root Vectors

The 21-root theorem counts ROOT CLASSES, not raw vectors.
Word length of a class = minimum word length of any representative.

A single root class can have representatives at MANY u-scales.
The w=0 family gives infinitely many representatives.
The w≥1 family gives representatives only at u ≥ 10⁴².

ROOT 19 (the first non-constructive root):
  Its class exists (Eichler-Kneser, H107).
  Its SHORTEST representative determines its word length.
  If the shortest representative has w ≥ 1, then |u| ≥ 10⁴².
  But the word length need not be 10⁴² — it depends on how the
  generator basis aligns with the eigenvector basis.

The word length 21 means: the 21st root class has minimum
representative word length 21 in the given generator set.
This is a property of the LATTICE GENERATORS, not just Q3p.
