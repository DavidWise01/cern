Panel 7 — Why It Looks Conformal (But Isn't)

The correction fractions fij = |ΔQij|/|Qij| depend on BOTH the numerator (rank-1 structure) and the denominator (Q magnitudes). The near-equality f00 ≈ f01 ≈ f11 comes from a cancellation between these two independent structures:

🍼 Toddler Version 🍼

Imagine you have a ball (the torroid) and it's pushing on a wobbly table.

The table can wobble two ways: it can SQUISH (the thin direction, like pressing a pancake flatter) or it can STRETCH (the wide direction, like pulling taffy).

We found out that when the ball pushes, it pushes mostly toward SQUISHING — about 73% squish and only 27% stretch!

So the ball is trying to make things flatter, not wider. It's like a heavy book sitting on a sandwich — it squishes down more than it pushes sideways.

But it's not pushing STRAIGHT down — it's tilted a little bit (31.57 degrees). So some sandwich squishes out the sides too.

And the really sneaky part? The push LOOKS almost the same in every direction (1.17% vs 1.20%), but that's just a coincidence! The push is actually as one-directional as it can possibly be — it just happens that the numbers almost cancel out when you look at the percentages.
