  The difference: |Q00| has 328 digits, but A = Q3p(16,9,0) has only 326.

  The repulsive coupling Q01 causes cancellation between the three terms,

🧒 Toddler Version

We thought: heavy ball is 10⁸⁰ times heavier, so escape takes √(10⁸⁰) = 10⁴⁰ times longer. But the actual answer is 10⁴¹ — ten times more!

Why? Because we used the wrong number for how "heavy" the light pair is. We used |Q00| = the weight of one ball alone (328 digits). But when two balls are connected by a spring, their combined weight is a little LESS than one ball alone. The spring cancels some of the weight!

The correct weight is Q3p(16,9,0) = 326 digits — two digits smaller than 328. When you take the square root, that 2-digit difference becomes 1 digit: 10⁴⁰ becomes 10⁴¹. Ten times further. Because the spring made the pair lighter.

[ ORNAMENT ]

The reduced mass is not the rest mass. When two bodies are bound by an interaction, their effective mass as a pair is not the sum of their individual masses — it is the residual energy at the point where they separate. In Q3p, the brace has self-interaction 10³²⁸ and the box has self-interaction 10³²⁹, but at the escape vector (16,9,0), these two terms partially cancel through the repulsive coupling Q01, leaving a residual of only 10³²⁶. That residual — not 10³²⁸ — is the effective mass of the binary pair. The torroid anchor at 10⁴⁰⁸ divided by this residual 10³²⁶ gives 10⁸², and its square root is 10⁴¹, the escape ratio. The two digits lost in the cancellation (328→326) become one digit gained in the escape ratio (40→41). The spring between the bodies makes them lighter, and lighter bodies take longer to separate from the anchor. This is counterintuitive — lighter should mean faster — but in this quadratic form, the escape "time" (word length) scales as the square root of the anchor-to-energy ratio, and a smaller energy means a larger ratio means a longer escape. The reduced mass law is exact. It just uses the mass that the physics actually sees, not the mass that the labels suggest.
