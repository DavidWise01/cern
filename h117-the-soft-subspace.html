H117 — The Soft Subspace

The z=0 plane reduces Q3p to a 2×2 form. Is it definite (no zeros) or indefinite (zeros exist)?

Panel 1 — The Question

H116 found the z=0 plane is the soft direction. Setting z=0 kills three terms (T3, T5, T6) and leaves a 2×2 quadratic form:

Q(x,y,0) = Q[0][0]·x² + 2·Q[0][1]·x·y + Q[1][1]·y²

This is the form defined by:

     M = [ Q[0][0]   Q[0][1] ]    = [ a   b ]
         [ Q[0][1]   Q[1][1] ]      [ b   c ]

where:
  a = Q[0][0] = 328 digits, NEGATIVE (self-coupling, brace body)
  b = Q[0][1] = 328 digits, POSITIVE (cross-coupling, brace↔box)
  c = Q[1][1] = 329 digits, NEGATIVE (self-coupling, box body)

The sign of det(M) = a·c − b² decides everything:

  det(M) > 0 → DEFINITE: no zeros possible, only closest approaches
  det(M) < 0 → INDEFINITE: zero directions EXIST
  det(M) = 0 → SINGULAR: a zero direction is a lattice direction

Toddler Version — The Two Magic Lines

Imagine a big flat field (the z=0 plane). Somewhere on this field, there are two invisible magic lines going through the middle. If you could stand EXACTLY on one of these lines, you'd weigh nothing — zero!

But here's the trick: these lines are tilted at weird angles that no whole number of steps can reach. You can walk 7 steps right and 4 steps up, or 9 right and 5 up — that gets you CLOSE to the magic line, but never exactly ON it.

The closest you can get is still 326 digits away from zero. That's like being 326 football fields from the finish line. You can see the magic line from where you're standing — you know it's there — but you can never step on it because your feet only land on whole-number grid points.

The eigenvalues are like two rulers measuring the field. One ruler is 329 digits long (the stiff direction). The other is 324 digits (the soft direction). Even the short ruler is unimaginably long. The ratio between them is about 324,000 — meaning the stiff direction is three hundred thousand times stiffer than the soft one.

IMPROVEMENT RECOMMENDATION → H118: The Irrational Barrier

H117 proves the z=0 restriction is indefinite — zeros exist on two lines of irrational slope. H118 would quantify the IRRATIONALITY MEASURE of these slopes: how well can rationals approximate them? The continued fraction coefficients bound the minimum |Q| achievable by integer vectors of given norm. If the slopes are badly approximable (bounded partial quotients), the barrier is permanent. If they have unbounded partial quotients, rare good approximations could break through — but only at astronomically large coordinates.
