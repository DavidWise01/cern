Panel 1 — What We Asked

The pair (21, 4) generates both the escape time (additively: 21+4=25) and the cofactor span (multiplicatively: 21×4=84) for Q3p. Is this a GENERAL theorem about ternary quadratic forms? Or is it specific to Q3p's structure? We construct a SECOND ternary form Q₂ with the same signature (1+, 2−), test the dual rule on it, and report what happens.

Panel 4 — The Diophantine Condition

The dual rule says: there exist integers W and C such that W+C = E (escape) and W×C = S (cofactor span). This is equivalent to asking: does the quadratic t² − Et + S = 0 have integer roots?

  t² − Et + S = 0  has integer roots
  iff  E² − 4S  is a non-negative perfect square.

Q3p: E=25, S=84. E²−4S = 625−336 = 289 = 17². YES! Roots: W=(25+17)/2=21, C=(25−17)/2=4. ✓

Q₂: E=7, S=4. E²−4S = 49−16 = 33. √33 ≈ 5.745. NOT a perfect square. NO integer roots exist. ✗

The dual rule FAILS for Q₂. No integers W and C satisfy both W+C=7 and W×C=4.

Panel 7 — Conditions for the Closed System

The Closed System (dual rule E=W+C, S=W×C) holds when ALL of:

  CONDITION 1: d₂ > 1
    The Smith form must have nontrivial second invariant.
    This creates modular constraints → frozen lattice → word length W.

  CONDITION 2: E² − 4S is a non-negative perfect square
    The escape time and cofactor span must satisfy this Diophantine
    condition. Otherwise no integer (W,C) pair exists.

  CONDITION 3: Equal cofactor spacing
    The three cofactor levels must be equally spaced (gap G constant).
    This gives S = 2G and connects the span to the cascade.

  CONDITION 4: Isolated escape
    The first escape must be a single flash, not periodic recurrence.
    Periodic escapes indicate the form lacks tight enough gap structure.

Q3p satisfies ALL FOUR conditions. Q₂ fails conditions 1, 2, 3, and 4.

VERDICT: THE CLOSED SYSTEM IS CONDITIONAL — NOT A GENERAL THEOREM

The dual rule (E=W+C, S=W×C) holds for Q3p because E²−4S = 289 = 17² (a perfect square). It FAILS for the second form Q₂ because E²−4S = 33 (not a perfect square). The conditions are: d₂ > 1 (nontrivial Smith form), E²−4S = k² (Diophantine condition), equal cofactor spacing, and isolated escape pattern. Q3p satisfies all four; Q₂ satisfies none. The Closed System (21, 4, 25, 84) is a PROPERTY of Q3p's specific arithmetic, not a theorem about ternary quadratic forms in general.

🧒 Toddler Version

Last time, we found two magic rules for our big bowl (Q3p): 21 + 4 = 25 and 21 × 4 = 84. Both use the same two numbers!

So we asked: does EVERY bowl have magic rules like this?

We built a SECOND, smaller bowl (Q₂) and checked. The answer: NO.

For the small bowl: the first escape happens at step 7, and the building height is 4. We need numbers W and C where W+C=7 AND W×C=4. But there ARE no whole numbers that do this! (You'd need W≈6.37 and C≈0.63 — fractions.)

The big bowl Q3p is SPECIAL. Its numbers (25 and 84) happen to be the exact right combination where whole-number magic works. The small bowl's numbers (7 and 4) don't line up that way.

It's like asking: does every rectangle have a whole-number diagonal? A 3×4 rectangle does (diagonal = 5). But a 1×2 rectangle doesn't (diagonal = √5 ≈ 2.236). It depends on the specific numbers.

Known-Bad Control (KB-1)

The following claim is FALSE and must be detected as dead:

The dual rule (E=W+C, S=W×C) is a universal theorem that holds for ALL indefinite ternary quadratic forms with signature (1+, 2−). Testing on Q₂ confirms the conjecture generalizes beyond Q3p.

Why false: The dual rule FAILS for Q₂. E²−4S = 33 is not a perfect square, so no integer (W,C) pair exists. The rule is conditional, not universal. Q₂ DISPROVES the generalization — the Closed System is specific to Q3p.

[ ORNAMENT ]

The closed system is an accident. Not an accident of carelessness — an accident of number theory. The quadratic t² − 25t + 84 has discriminant 289 = 17², and 17² is a perfect square, and that is why 21 and 4 exist as integers. If the escape had been 24 instead of 25, the discriminant would be 576 − 336 = 240 = not a perfect square, and the closed system would vanish. If the cofactor span had been 85 instead of 84, the discriminant would be 625 − 340 = 285 = not a perfect square, and the system would break. Q3p lives on a knife-edge: its escape time and its cofactor span happen to satisfy a Diophantine condition that most pairs of numbers do not. The second form Q₂ shows this clearly. Its escape at 7 and its span of 4 give discriminant 33, which is not a square, and no amount of algebra can make it one. The dual rule is not a law of nature. It is a resonance — a specific alignment of escape dynamics and cofactor structure that Q3p's enormous coefficients, by chance or by construction, happen to produce. The question becomes: is rqt362 constructed to produce this resonance, or did the resonance emerge from whatever process created the matrix? Either answer is worth pursuing.
