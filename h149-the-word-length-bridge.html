Panel 10 — Toddler Version: The Narrow Tunnel

🧒 Imagine a cone — like an ice cream cone, but made of invisible force. This cone is SUPER NARROW. It points along one direction (crack), and anything that wants to be a "root" has to touch this cone.

Now imagine a grid of dots (the lattice) filling all of space. Most dots miss the cone completely. But 21 dots land right on it — those are the roots.

18 of those dots are on the SIDE of the cone that faces Brace and Box. That side is wider (10⁻³ radians — small but findable). We found those 18 dots by searching.

3 more dots need the TORROID side of the cone. That side is SO narrow (10⁻⁴² radians!) that we can prove the dots exist but can't find them — the tunnel is too thin to look through. We know they're in there (Eichler and Kneser proved it), but finding them would mean factoring a 1056-digit number, which is as big as the whole lattice (det(Q) = 1059 digits).

The width of the tunnel? It's set by R01 — that same 651-digit number that's the root of everything.
