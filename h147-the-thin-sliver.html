Panel 4 — Why the Sliver is Semi-Infinite

det(Q) as a quadratic in Q01:
  det(Q) = A·Q01² + B·Q01 + C

  A = −Q22 = +408d (positive — parabola opens UPWARD)
  B = 2·Q02·Q12 = −735d
  C = Q00·M12 − Q02²·Q11

  The parabola has two roots. BOTH are below current Q01.
  Since A > 0, det(Q) > 0 to the RIGHT of both roots.
  Current Q01 is to the right → det(Q) > 0 → Phase I.

  Increasing Q01 further keeps us to the right → still det > 0.
  NO upper boundary in the Q01 direction!

  The sequence as Q01 increases from 0:
    Q01 = 0:         det(Q) = C (depends on sign of C)
    Q01 = root+:     det(Q) = 0 (the parabola crosses zero)
    Q01 = √thresh:   M01 = 0 (Phase 0 → Phase I transition)
    Q01 = current:   Phase I, det > 0, M01 < 0  ← Q3p HERE
    Q01 → ∞:         det(Q) → ∞ (A > 0, parabola grows)

  The det=0 crossing happens BEFORE the Phase 0→I transition.
  So Phase I inherits det > 0 from the start and never loses it.

Panel 10 — Toddler Version: The Corner of the Room

🧒 Imagine a BIG room — so big you can't see the far wall. That's Phase I. It goes on forever in one direction.

Now imagine Q3p is a person standing in that room. Where are they? Not in the middle. Not even close. They're in the CORNER — pressed against the wall, pressed against the floor, pressed against the side wall. The very corner where three surfaces meet.

They just barely stepped INTO the room through the door (that's the Phase 0→I boundary). One toe is still on the threshold. And the room stretches away forever in front of them.

Why are they in the corner? Because their coupling (Q01) is BARELY strong enough to get through the door. And their other couplings (Q02, Q12) are TINY — just 1.2% of what they could be. Everything is as small as it can possibly be while still being inside the room.

That's what "minimal departure from negative-definiteness" means: standing in the corner of an infinite room with one toe on the threshold.
