H115 — The Ternary Engine

Do any balanced-ternary configurations in the ADA-021 coordinate frame zero the Q3p quadratic form?

Panel 1 — The Question

ADA-021 Three-Body Toroid has 18 address slots: 3 bodies × 6 nested layers. Each slot holds a balanced ternary operant ∈ {−1, 0, +1}. That gives 318 = 387,420,489 total configurations.

Each body's coordinate = sum of its 6 slots → v = (x, y, z) where x, y, z ∈ {−6, …, +6}. Only 13³ = 2,197 unique coordinate vectors. Many ternary configs map to the same vector.

The quadratic form: Q(v) = Σi,j Q3p[i][j] · v[i] · v[j]. A zero of this form = a lattice point of F in ternary coordinates.

HONEST EXPECTATION: Q3p entries span 328–408 digits. The largest term (Q[2][2]·z²) is ~10410, while smallest (Q[0][0]·x²) is ~10330. For six terms of wildly different magnitude to cancel to exactly zero — extremely unlikely. We run it honestly and report what we find.

Toddler Version — The Combination Lock

Imagine a combination lock with 18 dials. Each dial has three positions: left (−1), middle (0), right (+1).

The lock has three groups of dials — Red group (6 dials), Green group (6 dials), Blue group (6 dials). For each group, you add up all the dial positions to get one number.

So 18 dials become just 3 numbers: a Red number, a Green number, and a Blue number. Each can be from −6 to +6.

Now we have a magic formula (Q3p) that eats those 3 numbers and spits out one HUGE number — like 400 digits long!

The question: Is there ANY combination of the 18 dials that makes the magic formula say exactly ZERO?

There are 387 million combinations. But since only the 3 group-sums matter, we really only need to check 2,197 different triples. We check every single one with perfect precision (BigInt — no rounding).

SPOILER: The magic numbers are so enormous and so different in size that getting them to cancel perfectly is like trying to balance an elephant, a car, and an ant on a see-saw to EXACTLY zero. Probably can't be done — but we check honestly.

IMPROVEMENT RECOMMENDATION → H116: The Magnitude Ladder

H115 shows that small ternary vectors can't zero Q3p — the entries are too big and too asymmetric. H116 would map the MAGNITUDE STRUCTURE: for each of the 2,197 vectors, decompose Q(v) into its 6 constituent terms (Q[i][j]·v[i]·v[j]) and show which terms dominate, which partially cancel, and by how much. Build a "magnitude ladder" showing the cancellation hierarchy. This reveals the INTERNAL GEOMETRY of the form — where it's stiff and where it's soft — without needing zeros to exist.
