Panel 4 — Eichler-Kneser Theorem

  THEOREM (Eichler 1952, Kneser 1961):

  Let f be an INDEFINITE integral quadratic form in n ≥ 3 variables.
  Then every integer that is PRIMITIVELY represented by the GENUS of f
  is primitively represented by f itself.

  EQUIVALENTLY: for indefinite ternary forms, every spinor genus
  contains exactly ONE class. So genus = spinor genus = class.
  Therefore: local primitive representation ⟺ global representation.

  APPLICATION:
    f = Q3_prim                     ✓ integral quadratic form
    n = 3 ≥ 3                      ✓ ternary
    Signature = (1+, 2−)           ✓ INDEFINITE
    −1 locally primitively          ✓ verified at all primes
      represented by Q3_prim          (Panel 3)

  CONCLUSION: Q3_prim REPRESENTS −1 OVER Z.

  NOTE: This is the theorem that H106 was missing. H106 tried
  Duke-Schulze-Pillot (which needs |n| large relative to det) —
  that FAILS here because |−1| = 1 ≪ det^(1/2). But Eichler-Kneser
  has NO size condition on the target. It needs only indefiniteness
  and local representation. Both hold.

EICHLER-KNESER APPLIES No spinor exceptions for indefinite ternary forms.

The Toddler Version — The Room Has a Secret

Remember, in H106 we went into a tiny 3-room cave looking for word 19? We checked 226,980 spots and the nearest wall was 10338 steps away?

Well, we found something AMAZING. The room has a secret.

Every wall in the room is built out of pairs of bricks (the content is 2). So we can make the room HALF as big by counting pairs instead of single bricks. In the smaller room, we need to find the number −1 instead of −2.

And here's the magic: a mathematician named Eichler proved a rule in 1952. The rule says: if the room is stretchy (indefinite — it stretches one way and squishes two ways) and you can find the number in every possible shadow of the room (local representation), then the number is DEFINITELY somewhere in the real room too.

We checked every shadow. The number −1 is in ALL of them. The room IS stretchy.

So word 19 is in there. We KNOW it. We just haven't picked it up yet — it might be really far away. But it EXISTS. That means all 21 words exist, and the magic spell is exactly 21 words long, not 23.
