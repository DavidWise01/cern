Panel 2 — 3×C² = OCHO × entries = 48

  The BRIDGE: entries = bodies × d₁ = 3 × 2 = 6.

Panel 3 — entries = bodies × d₁

Why do the two factorizations of 48 agree? Because of one identity:

  entries = bodies × d₁ = 3 × 2 = 6

  Proof: T(n) = n(n+1)/2. At n=3: T(3) = 3×4/2 = 6.
         bodies × d₁ = n × 2 = 3 × 2 = 6.
         T(3) = bodies × d₁ ⟺ n(n+1)/2 = 2n
                              ⟺ (n+1)/2 = 2
                              ⟺ n+1 = 4
                              ⟺ n = 3.

This identity holds only at n=3. At n=2: T(2)=3 but bodies×d₁=2×2=4. At n=4: T(4)=10 but bodies×d₁=4×2=8. Only at n=3 do they coincide.

entries = bodies × d₁ is the bridge that lets boundary identifications equal OCHO × entries. It is structural, it is specific to our dimension, and it was hiding in plain arithmetic.

I109: entries = bodies × d₁ = 6. Bridge identity. Valid only at n=3. [LIT]

Panel 4 — Faces = Entries Only at n = 3

A cube in n dimensions has 2n faces. A symmetric n×n matrix has T(n) = n(n+1)/2 entries.

  n=1: faces=2,  entries=1.  ≠
  n=2: faces=4,  entries=3.  ≠
  n=3: faces=6,  entries=6.  ═══ EQUAL ═══
  n=4: faces=8,  entries=10. ≠
  n=5: faces=10, entries=15. ≠
  n=6: faces=12, entries=21. ≠

  2n = n(n+1)/2  ⟺  4n = n² + n  ⟺  n = 3.

At n=3 and only n=3: the number of faces of the box equals the number of independent entries in the symmetric matrix. This is why Q3p (a 3×3 symmetric form with 6 entries) can be read as a cube (6 faces).

This is the deepest uniqueness result since the adjugate fixed point (H224). Both say: n=3 is special.

I110: faces(cube) = entries(symmetric matrix) ⟺ n=3. The unique dimension. [LIT]

🧸 Toddler Version

Remember the fence from last time? 4 sticks, 5 spaces, makes a yard.

To wrap it into a donut, you need to glue the walls together: left↔right, front↔back, top↔bottom. That's 48 glue spots (16 per pair of walls × 3 pairs).
