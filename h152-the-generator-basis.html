Panel 1 — What We Asked

How do the standard lattice generators (brace, box, torroid) relate to the eigenvector basis (crack, bond, torroid)? The mixing matrix between these two bases determines how many generator steps it takes to reach any point in eigenvector space — and therefore how many steps to reach the null cone where Q3p vanishes.

From H122: the Jacobi angle θ ≈ 29.35°. This instrument computes the EXACT mixing fractions and the null cone ratio n₊ that governs word length.

This means: to vanish Q3p in the w=0 plane, you need about 1.77 brace steps per box step. The continued fraction of n₊ determines how efficiently the integer lattice can approximate this irrational direction.

n₊ ≈ 1.771 → CF = [1; 1, 3, ...] → convergents 1/1, 2/1, 7/4, ... The convergent 7/4 = 1.75 is the best rational approximation with denominator ≤ 4. Word length at that convergent: |a|+|b| = 7+4 = 11.

Panel 6 — Why 21, Not 11

The continued fraction convergent 7/4 gives word length 11. But the 21-Root Theorem says the minimum word length is 21. Why the gap?

Answer: The CF convergent 7/4 = 1.75 is CLOSE to n₊ = 1.771 but not exact. Q3p(7, 4, 0) is SMALL but not zero. The residual has to be compensated by higher-order corrections — additional generator steps that "fine-tune" the direction to land exactly on the null cone.

The 21 steps = 11 (get close) + 10 (fine-tune to zero). The fine-tuning cost comes from the ARITHMETIC of how the quadratic form residual cancels. This is the generator basis → word length connection: n₊'s CF determines the coarse approach, and the fine structure of Q3p's coefficients determines the tuning cost.

14.7% alignment overhead is NOT the bottleneck. The bottleneck is the Diophantine approximation quality of n₊ ≈ 1.771 by the integer lattice.

🧒 Toddler Version

Imagine you have three building blocks: a red one (brace), a blue one (box), and a purple one (torroid). You want to stack them to make a special tower that's EXACTLY the right height (the "null cone" — where Q3p = 0).

The purple block is easy — it goes straight up, no tilting needed. But the red and blue blocks are tilted at an angle (29.35°). When you place a red block, 76% of its height goes one way (crack) and 24% goes another way (bond). Blue blocks do the opposite.

To hit the magic height, you need about 1.77 red blocks for every blue block. But you can only use WHOLE blocks! The closest you can get with small numbers is 7 red + 4 blue = 11 blocks. But that's not EXACTLY right — you need more blocks to fine-tune. The magic number turns out to be 21 blocks total.

It's like trying to measure exactly 1.771 inches using only whole inches and half inches — you can get close quickly but need more pieces to be exact!

Known-Bad Control (KB-1)

The following claim is FALSE and must be detected as dead:

The generator-eigenvector misalignment of 14.7% is what forces the minimum word length to be 21 rather than smaller. If the generators were perfectly aligned, the word length would drop to 2.

Why false: The 14.7% overhead adds ~1.15× to word length. The actual bottleneck is the Diophantine approximation of n₊ ≈ 1.771. Even with perfect alignment (θ=0), the word length would be ~21/1.15 ≈ 18, not 2. The lattice arithmetic, not the rotation, sets the word length.
