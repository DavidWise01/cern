Panel 3 — Why the Factor of 2

The cofactor gap G = 42 measures a 2D quantity (the ratio of two 2×2 minor determinants). The word length measures a 1D quantity (the ℓ¹ path length through the lattice).

  DIMENSIONAL ANALYSIS:

  [cofactor] = [2×2 determinant] = [length]²  (area-like)
  [word length] = [ℓ¹ path] = [length]        (length-like)

  Conversion: length = √area

  cof₁₁/cof₀₂ ≈ 10^G = 10^42  (ratio of areas)
  √(10^42) = 10^21              (ratio of lengths)

  The EXPONENT halves: G → G/2
  The WORD LENGTH = G/2 = 42/2 = 21

The factor of 2 is NOT arbitrary — it is the dimensional conversion from area (cofactors, which are 2×2 determinants) to length (word length, which is a 1D path measure). This is the same √ that converts discriminant to shortest vector in classical lattice theory.

🧒 Toddler Version

Our three-floor building has 42 steps between each floor. When you measure a floor in AREA (like painting it), you count 42. But when you measure the SHORTEST PATH across it (like walking), you count √42 ≈ half of 42 = 21.

Areas are SQUARES of lengths. When you take the square root, the number of digits cuts in half. 42 digits of area → 21 digits of length. That's why the building has 42-step floors but the shortest path takes 21 steps.

The number 21 shows up FIVE different ways — in the maze length, in the root count, in the floor gap, in a special math sequence, and in adding up some special fractions. All five paths lead to the same door: 21.
