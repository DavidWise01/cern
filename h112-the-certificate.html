The Deliverable Form


A theorem is a claim. A proof is an argument. A certificate is neither — it is the thing you hand someone and say: check. Here are the numbers. All of them. Nine entries of a 3×3 matrix, each hundreds of digits long. Four kernel basis vectors. A determinant with 1058 digits and one known factor. The existence conditions. The rank descent. The terminal uniqueness. Everything that makes the number 21 the answer. The certificate does not persuade. It does not explain. It sits there, 50 kilobytes of exact decimal strings, and says: compute. If you get 21, the theorem holds. If you don't, something is wrong, and the certificate tells you exactly where to look. That is the difference between a story about a wall and the wall itself.
