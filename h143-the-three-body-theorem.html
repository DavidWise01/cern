Panel 3 — The Three-Body Theorem (Corrected)

THEOREM. Let Q be a 3×3 real symmetric matrix with:
  (a) all diagonal entries negative: Qii < 0 for all i
  (b) exactly one 2×2 principal minor negative: M₀₁ < 0, M₀₂ > 0, M₁₂ > 0
  (c) det(Q) > 0

Then for the scaled Schur complements S_k_scaled:

  (i)   ALL THREE have negative determinant.
        PROOF: det(S_k) = det(Q)/Qkk. Since det(Q) > 0 and Qkk < 0,
        det(S_k) < 0 for all k. ∎

  (ii)  For k ∈ {0,1} (sick pair MEMBERS):
        S_k_scaled has one NEGATIVE and one POSITIVE diagonal entry.
        The negative entry is M₀₁ (the sick minor).
        CS(S_k) < 1 numerically for Q3p (0.0865 and 0.0868).

  (iii) For k = 2 (NON-MEMBER):
        S_k_scaled has two POSITIVE diagonal entries (M₀₂, M₁₂ > 0).
        CS(S_k) > 1 UNCONDITIONALLY.
        PROOF: det(S_2_scaled) = M₀₂·M₁₂ − X₂₂² < 0 (from (i)).
        Therefore X₂₂² > M₀₂·M₁₂, i.e. CS > 1. ∎

NOTE: Part (ii) CS < 1 is NUMERICAL for Q3p. Part (iii) CS > 1 is PROVED.

The sign rule: The sick minor M01 < 0 appears as a diagonal in EXACTLY the member pivots (k=0, k=1). The non-member pivot (k=2) has ONLY the two positive minors as diagonals. When a negative minor is on the diagonal, the matrix is indefinite by STRUCTURE (opposite signs, regardless of coupling). When both diagonals are positive, the matrix is indefinite only if the coupling exceeds the Cauchy-Schwarz bound — and part (iii) proves it does.

Panel 6 — Proof of Part (iii): CS > 1 at Non-Member Pivot

Given: conditions (a)(b)(c) of the theorem.

The scaled Schur complement of Q22 (the non-member):
  S_2_scaled = [[M02, X₂₂], [X₂₂, M12]]

Its determinant:
  det(S_2_scaled) = Q22 · det(Q)        [standard Schur identity]
                  = (negative) · (positive)   [by (a) and (c)]
                  = NEGATIVE

Also:
  det(S_2_scaled) = M02 · M12 − X₂₂²   [2×2 determinant formula]

Since M02 > 0 and M12 > 0 [by (b)]:
  M02 · M12 > 0

Since det(S_2_scaled) < 0:
  M02 · M12 − X₂₂² < 0
  X₂₂² > M02 · M12

Therefore:
  CS(S_2) = X₂₂² / (M02 · M12) > 1     ∎

This proof uses ONLY:
  - All diagonal entries negative (a)
  - M02, M12 > 0 (b, partially)
  - det(Q) > 0 (c)
  - The Schur determinant identity

No reference to specific values. No numerics. Pure algebra.

Panel 9 — Toddler Version: Two Kinds of Wobbly

🧒 Imagine a seesaw. A seesaw is WOBBLY — one side goes up, the other goes down. That's "indefinite" in math language.

Now there are two ways a seesaw can be wobbly:

Kind 1: One side is heavy, the other is light. No matter what you put in the middle, it's going to tip. The heaviness and lightness are BUILT IN. That's what happens when the sick minor M01 is on the diagonal — one side is negative, the other is positive. The seesaw HAS to tip.

Kind 2: Both sides are the same weight, but someone pushes too hard. Both sides COULD balance — they're the same kind (both positive). But the push (the off-diagonal coupling) is too strong, so it tips anyway. That's what happens at the non-member pivot — both diags are positive, but the coupling exceeds the Cauchy-Schwarz bound.

ALL three seesaws tip. But the first two tip because they were BUILT wobbly, and the third tips because it was PUSHED over. The theorem proves the push always happens — it's not a coincidence.
