Panel 4 — Ostrowski Representation of 21

Ostrowski's theorem: every positive integer has a unique representation n = Σ cₖ · qₖ where qₖ are CF convergent denominators and 0 ≤ cₖ ≤ aₖ₊₁ (with the constraint that if cₖ = aₖ₊₁ then cₖ₋₁ = 0).

  Convergent denominators: q₀=1, q₁=1, q₂=4, q₃=9, q₄=13, q₅=35

  OSTROWSKI(21):
  21 = 0·q₀ + 0·q₁ + 2·q₂ + 0·q₃ + 1·q₄
     = 0×1  + 0×1  + 2×4  + 0×9  + 1×13
     = 0    + 0    + 8    + 0    + 13
     = 8 + 13 = 21  ✓

  Ostrowski digits: [0, 0, 2, 0, 1]
  Active denominators: q₂ = 4  and  q₄ = 13
  Corresponding convergents: 7/4 and 23/13

FINDING: The word length 21 is built from EXACTLY the two best small rational approximations to n₊ ≈ 1.771. The convergent 7/4 = 1.75 (q₂=4, used twice) and the convergent 23/13 = 1.769 (q₄=13, used once). These are the same convergents identified in H153 as the closest lattice approaches to the null cone.

🧒 Toddler Version

Imagine you're counting steps on a staircase, and each step has a different size (1, 1, 3, 2, 1, 2, 4, 7, ...). After step 8, you've climbed exactly 21 stairs total (1+1+3+2+1+2+4+7 = 21). That's the magic number!

Now, there's another way to make 21: use two special measuring sticks. One stick is 4 units long, the other is 13. Put down TWO short sticks and ONE long stick: 4+4+13 = 21. These measuring sticks come from the best guesses at the magic ratio 1.771 — the guesses 7/4 and 23/13.

So 21 isn't just a random number — it's built from the two best guesses at the magic ratio. Two different paths (stair-counting and stick-measuring) both lead to exactly 21.

Known-Bad Control (KB-1)

The following claim is FALSE and must be detected as dead:

The Smith invariant d₂ = 18 divides the word length 21, confirming that 21 is a Smith-form-derived lattice property. The quotient 21/18 gives the index of the frozen lattice in the full integer lattice.

Why false: 21 mod 18 = 3 ≠ 0. The Smith invariant d₂ does NOT divide 21. The word length is not a Smith-derived quantity. It is built from CF convergent denominators through the Ostrowski representation, not from divisibility properties of the matrix invariants.
