Two numbers with 656 digits each agree on their first four.

This precision doesn't arise from coincidence.
It arises from ALGEBRAIC STRUCTURE.

THE CAUCHY-SCHWARZ CONNECTION:
  For positive definite matrices:
    |M[i][j]|² ≤ M[i][i] · M[j][j]    (always)
    ρ² = |M[i][j]|² / (M[i][i]·M[j][j]) ≤ 1

  For INDEFINITE matrices (like Q3p):
    The inequality CAN FLIP: ρ² > 1
    When ρ² > 1, the restriction is INDEFINITE
    When ρ² < 1, the restriction is DEFINITE

  Q3p has THREE correlation coefficients:
    ρ²(brace,box)     ≈ 1.000017  → z=0 INDEFINITE (barely flipped)
    ρ²(brace,torroid)  ≈ 0.012    → y=0 DEFINITE (safely below 1)
    ρ²(box,torroid)    ≈ 0.012    → x=0 DEFINITE (safely below 1)

H120 investigates: what forces ρ²(brace,box) so close to 1?

Toddler Version — The Almost-Twins

Brace and Box are like twins. They look almost EXACTLY alike — 99.9992% identical. When you multiply their self-portraits together (how brace sees brace, times how box sees box), you get a big number. When you square their HANDSHAKE (how brace sees box), you get another big number.

These two numbers are SO close that they agree on their first four digits: 5-7-2-3. Then comes the FIFTH digit — and the handshake-number has a 6 where the portrait-number has a 5.

That tiny difference — one digit out of 656 — is what makes the z=0 plane wobbly. It's like two kids who weigh almost exactly the same trying to balance a seesaw. One kid is 17 grams heavier out of a million grams. You can barely tell. But the seesaw tips.

Meanwhile, Torroid is NOT a twin of either one. Its "handshake" with brace or box is only 1.2% as strong as it could be. It's the kid from another town who doesn't even look like the twins. When Torroid is in the mix, it dominates — the seesaw is locked down. Only when both twins are alone together does the wobble show.
