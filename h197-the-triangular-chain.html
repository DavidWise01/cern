Panel 3 — The Chain Is a Construction Sequence

  Read the main chain as a CONSTRUCTION:

  STEP 1: Start with d₁ = 2 (the matrix GCD).
           "What is the smallest shared factor?"

  STEP 2: T(2) = 3 bodies.
           "How many things can interact pairwise,
            given a GCD of 2?" → 3 bodies.

  STEP 3: T(3) = 6 entries.
           "How many unique pairwise interactions
            among 3 bodies?" → 6 entries.

  STEP 4: T(6) = 21 = W.
           "How many word-length slots does a 6-entry
            form need before the lattice freezes?" → 21.

  Each step answers: "given n things, how many pairs?"
  T(n) = n(n+1)/2 = the number of pairs including self-pairs.

  d₁ → T → bodies → T → entries → T → frozen boundary.
  The triangular function IS the step from one level to the next.

🧒 Toddler Version

Triangular numbers count PAIRS. If you have 3 friends and everyone shakes hands with everyone (including yourself), that's 3×4÷2 = 6 handshakes.

Now watch this chain:

Start with 2 (the matrix's GCD — its simplest building block).

"How many handshakes with 2 people?" → 3. And 3 is the number of bodies!

"How many handshakes with 3 people?" → 6. And 6 is the number of matrix entries!

"How many handshakes with 6 people?" → 21. And 21 is the frozen word length!

FOUR things, each built from the last one by counting handshakes. The matrix's most basic number (2) builds up to its most important one (21) in exactly three steps of pair-counting.

Bonus: "handshakes with 4 people" = 10 = the QCD count! "Handshakes with 8 people" = 36 = the gcd of the minors!
